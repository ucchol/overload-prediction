## Corrections and flags

- Item 1. The author is A. R. (Ray) Pike. Volume 80, issue 1, pages 53–68 were confirmed on library and index pages and in a reference list. The DOI 10.1037/h0033871 follows the APA pattern for that period but could not be confirmed on a primary page.
- Item 3. The title is "Time-dependent Poisson counter models of response latency in simple judgment". The paper's contribution is time-varying (inhomogeneous) rates, so do not describe it as a time-homogeneous model. Cite it as the general Poisson counter framework, of which ours is the homogeneous, criterion 2 case. Volume 53(2), 293–315 was confirmed from a published reference list, and the DOI from the publisher record.
- Item 4. The DOI and PubMed ID were confirmed. The issue (1) and pages (37–58) could not be confirmed on a primary page. Vickers's accumulator sums evidence magnitudes, so it is not a count (Poisson) model.
- Item 6. The aDDM assumes fixations independent of value. If the paper says the aDDM has value-driven attention, that is wrong.
- Item 12. The title is "Uncovering the computational mechanisms underlying many-alternative choice". "Probabilistic satisficing" is correct and is the name of a model they test (adapted from Reutskaja et al. 2011).
- Item 13. The article number is e1008863. Only binary and trinary choice are modelled.
- Everything else matched what was listed.

The "horse race" random utility model for choice probabilities and reaction times, and its competing risks interpretation. *Journal of Mathematical Psychology*, 36(1), 1–20. | 10.1016/0022-2496(92)90050-H (unconfirmed) | A formal treatment of choice as a race among independent finishing times, linking choice probabilities and response times. Our model is a horse race whose horses have Erlang(2) finishing times. This is the right citation for the race-as-random-utility view. Details were confirmed from a published reference list only. |
| A2 | Partial (DOI not confirmed) | Bundesen, C. (1990). A theory of visual attention. *Psychological Review*, 97(4), 523–547. | 10.1037/0033-295X.97.4.523 (unconfirmed) | TVA. Each candidate races with an exponential rate set by its attentional weight, and the first to finish is selected. This gives a Luce (softmax) choice rule. That is our model with threshold 1, so it marks exactly what the second look adds. |
| A3 | Y | Shimojo, S., Simion, C., Shimojo, E., & Scheier, C. (2003). Gaze bias both reflects and influences preference. *Nature Neuroscience*, 6(12), 1317–1322. | 10.1038/nn1150 | The gaze cascade. In two-face preference choice, gaze is evenly split at first and then shifts more and more to the face that will be chosen. Manipulating gaze shifts preference. It supports the idea that looks concentrate on the eventual choice near the end, as when the winner collects its second look. |
| A4 | Y | Russo, J. E., & Leclerc, F. (1994). An eye-fixation analysis of choice processes for consumer nondurables. *Journal of Consumer Research*, 21(2), 274–290. | 10.1086/209397 | Supermarket shelf simulation with eye tracking. Choice runs in three stages (orientation, evaluation with repeated comparisons of two or three products, verification). This is evidence that choice involves returning to a small set of options. Caution, the abstract says the verification stage mainly looked at alternatives with few or no earlier fixations, so it does not show that the final act is a refixation of the chosen item. |
| A5 | Y | Camarri, M., & Pitman, J. (2000). Limit distributions and random trees derived from the birthday problem with unequal probabilities. *Electronic Journal of Probability*, 5, paper 2, 1–18. | 10.1214/EJP.v5-58 | Gives exact and asymptotic distributions of the first repeat time (and later repeats) when sampling with replacement from unequal probabilities, using a Poisson embedding. This is the nonuniform birthday problem behind our search length. It grounds the square-root growth, which in the classical result is a first repeat near sqrt(π / (2 Σ p_i²)), or about sqrt(πN/2) for equal probabilities. The journal page gives the online date as 16 November 1999, and volume 5 is the 2000 volume, so either year appears in citations. |

Source of the relation sentences. Most come from the abstract or full text seen during this check. Four rest partly on general knowledge and should be checked against the paper itself. These are Pike (the abstract confirms only "two contrasting models" for yes/no latency, not which two), Marley and Colonius, Bundesen, and the square-root formula given for Camarri and Pitman (the abstract confirms exact and asymptotic first repeat formulas with Rayleigh-type limits, not the formula itself).

No prior paper was found that uses "the first option looked at twice is chosen" as a preferential choice rule. Searches for refixation-based stopping in multi-alternative choice turned up only the gaze-weighted and satisficing models above. This supports a novelty claim, but it should be worded as "we are not aware of".
